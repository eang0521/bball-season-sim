# Season Progression Rules

This is the rulebook behind `src/season/`, ported from a spreadsheet model
(FBA) of 24 simulated NBA seasons. It runs once per team-year, between one
season's final roster and the next season's opening-day roster.

## Player state

Every player carries:

- **Ratings**: `twoPt`, `threePt`, `def`, `reb` — the four skills `nba-game-sim`'s
  engine already uses (see `../nba-game-sim/RULEBOOK.md`).
- **Overall** — the player's current rating, `= twoPt + threePt + def + reb`.
- **Potential** — a ceiling Overall is chasing. Potential is not fixed: it
  drifts on its own, and erodes with age.
- **Age**, **salary**, **yearsLeft** (on contract), **yearsWithTeam**.

## The offseason, step by step

1. **Retirement.** Roll `1d6 * 10` (10–60). A player retires if the roll
   beats their Overall *and* their Overall has already caught up to (or
   passed) their Potential. Potential decays with age (see step 4), so
   retirement risk emerges naturally once a player is past the point where
   they still have room to grow.

2. **Free agency.** Survivors on an expiring (1-year) contract roll
   `0d89`. If that roll beats their team's win total from the season just
   played (capped at 88), they hit free agency instead of re-signing in
   place. Bad teams bleed their expiring talent; good teams tend to keep it.

3. **Matching free agents to teams.** Every free agent (departures *and*
   incoming rookies — see step 5) gets a value score dominated by Potential,
   with Overall and youth as tiebreakers, and is ranked best to worst.
   Every opening (one per retirement, one per departure) gets a "need"
   score: lower payroll ranks needier, and a team's 2nd/3rd/... opening
   always ranks behind every team's 1st. Pair the lists top to top — best
   remaining player to neediest remaining opening — all the way down. There
   is no explicit salary cap; this is the whole free-agency/bidding model.

4. **Aging and development.** Every player who didn't retire ages one year.
   Overall moves about halfway toward Potential, plus a small random walk
   (`±1d6`). Potential moves about halfway toward *its own* projected
   change, plus another small random walk, **minus a penalty that grows by
   1 roughly every 4 years past age 16** — this is the aging curve, and it's
   what eventually lets Overall overtake a shrinking Potential and trigger
   retirement. The resulting change is split across the four ratings in the
   same direction (all up or all down together), in random proportions.

5. **Drafting replacements.** Count this offseason's retirements — that
   many new prospects are generated and funneled into the exact same
   free-agent matching step as any departing veteran. A prospect isn't a
   blank slate: it's generated by rolling an amateur baseline at a random
   draft-eligible age (15–18) and then playing it forward through a few
   years of pre-draft development (the same aging/development formula from
   step 4, run a few times) — so draft classes already have some spread of
   busts, sleepers, and sure things, the way real ones do.

6. **Contracts.** Anyone whose contract just expired (including newly
   signed free agents and rookies on their rookie-scale deal) gets a new
   salary pegged to whichever of Overall/Potential is lower, and a new
   1–5-year length. Everyone else just ticks their years-left down by one.

Net effect: the league stays a fixed size (retirements in == rookies in),
and every knob above lives in `src/season/config.js` so a "mode" is just a
different config passed into the same engine.
